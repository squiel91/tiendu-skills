# Campaign audit

## Scope and data

1. Confirm account, goal, campaign(s), period and currency. Use a comparable period with enough activity; with little activity, state the uncertainty.
2. Read the structure and states of campaign, ad sets and ads. Look for recent changes, budget, optimization, audience, placements, frequency and destination where the tools allow.
3. Get performance with suitable dates and breakdowns: spend, impressions, reach, frequency, link clicks, landing page views, conversions, cost per result, value and ROAS when available. Check definitions and attribution window before comparing.
4. Inspect creatives and the landing page. To compare images visually, follow [ads](ads.md). Cross-check Pixel and catalog problems with [measurement](measurement.md) and [catalog](catalog.md).

## Diagnosis

Split the funnel: delivery → attention → visit → intent → purchase. Find where it drops and what evidence supports the hypothesis. Clicks without visits can point to the destination or load time; visits without purchases to the offer, the store or measurement. Do not give one cause for an isolated number.

Compare ads with the same goal and similar conditions. Separate exploration from stable results; an ad with few conversions does not justify a strong conclusion. Consider margin and customer value before calling CPA or ROAS good or bad. Avoid fixed rules such as "frequency > 3 means fatigue".

## Report to the seller

- Summarize goal and period, spend and observed results, with source and limits.
- Rank up to three findings by likely impact and confidence.
- Give one concrete action per finding, its risk and how to measure whether it helped. Change one variable per test when possible and agree a review period.
- If events, margin, catalog or enough data are missing, say so and ask only for what would change the decision.
